Write music for the Sega Mega Drive

Or just make music with the same limits the console had. The YM2612, the SN76489 and the sample channel are the instruments, and the hardware stays in front of you the whole time.

The hardware stays in view

Draw a note the chip can't sound and it's hatched in the piano roll straight away, with a warning that clicks through to the note that caused it. Hover an FM parameter to see the register it writes and the raw value.

What you hear is what you export

One part of the program makes every register write. Playback, the export, the register timeline and the hardware meters all read that same stream, so nothing can quietly drift.

Small, with nothing to install

An 8 MB program next to a copy of SDL. No runtime, no framework, no .NET, no Electron and no Java. Download it and run it, or keep the portable copy on a USB stick.

Composing

Write the way you already do

Patterns on a playlist, a piano roll with lanes underneath it, and a tracker view of the same notes in hexadecimal rows. If you've used FL Studio, most of it will feel familiar.

  • A playlist of clips over named tracks, each with a colour and an icon
  • A scale and key, snap, and zoom to fit
  • Plug in a MIDI keyboard to play or record the selected channel
  • Undo and redo on everything, drags included
Sound design

An FM editor that shows the wiring

Every operator has its envelope drawn and its values in reach, and the algorithm is drawn as separate wires. Automation lanes write straight to the chip's registers.

  • Preset banks you can search, group, sort and filter
  • A square editor beside the FM one
  • Automation points that curve into the next point
Watching the hardware

See what the console sees

A scope for every channel, a register timeline that says which chip took each write, and a meter showing how much of the console the song uses.

  • Waveform or spectrum in the scope
  • Three output stages: the chip alone, the Mega Drive or the Mega Drive 2
  • Play through a driver, paced the way the console's own sound driver would
In and out

Bring music in, and get it back out

Import a VGM and get notes, presets, square envelopes and samples back, with the timing, vibrato and slides kept as the file wrote them. Export for the console or for everyone else.

  • Import VGM, XGM, MIDI, WAV and TFI
  • Export VGM, XGM and MIDI
  • Audio as WAV, FLAC, Ogg Vorbis or Opus, and stems that sum back to the mix
  • A video of the scope with the mix as its sound, ready for YouTube
